Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Springdale

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ACSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Call Both TradesCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • The drain pipe for condensate blocked by a buildup of dust and algae.
  • Water escaping from a drain pan that has cracked, rusted through or tilted.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Mold Around the Air Handler

Mold may appear in 24 to 48 hours on damp materials by the air handler, where the return air stream can carry its spores through the ductwork to other rooms.

Ceilings Under the Unit

Overflow from a unit in the attic lands on the ceiling drywall. First comes a stain, next a sag, and after days of soaking a piece may drop into the bedroom or hall underneath.

Attic Insulation

Insulation around an attic unit soaks up overflow and holds it against the ceiling. Once saturated it stops insulating, and replacement is common.

Baseboards and Door Trim

The bottom of the door jamb and the baseboard beside it take on water early. Fiberboard trim swells and crumbles, so it is usually replaced, not dried.

Insurance

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • The number of days water kept dripping before the AC was turned off.
  • The location of the air handler, since attic, garage and closet leaks spread differently.
  • Mold growth, if any, and the containment and removal it calls for.
  • Rot in the stand beneath the air handler, which can mean rebuilding it.
