Few water damage events are as common, or as potentially devastating to a property, as a pipe that bursts. A single burst pipe can release 4 to 8 gallons of water per minute, which is 240 to 480 gallons per hour flooding through your walls, ceilings and floors. Pipes burst from freezing, old plumbing, too much water pressure, corrosion or a physical hit, and in every case a fast emergency response is what keeps water from ruining flooring, structural materials, building systems and your belongings.

Day or night, Springdale Restoration responds to burst pipe emergencies across Springdale and the nearby communities. Our restoration technicians can help you locate and shut off your water supply, and we coordinate with licensed plumbers so the plumbing repair is done properly. Once the water source is stopped, we immediately begin extraction with truck-mounted and portable equipment, removing standing water from all affected areas as quickly as possible.

After the water is out, we dry every material that took on moisture: finished floors, subflooring, wood framing, insulation and drywall. Burst pipes often damage multiple rooms and multiple levels, because water follows gravity through wall cavities, floor assemblies and ceiling spaces. Hidden moisture leads to mold and a deteriorating structure, so we use moisture detection equipment and thermal imaging to map the water's path and make sure all affected areas get dried.

Signs It Is Time for Burst Pipe Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Damp, moldy odors around sinks, tubs or walls that hide pipes
  • Water charges that jump for no clear reason, a sign of a concealed leak
  • Hearing water move through the house while nothing is turned on
  • Rusty or discolored water coming from your taps
  • Ceilings or walls that show damp patches, stains or a swollen bulge
  • Water pooling on floors with no apparent source
  • A spray or steady drip coming off pipework you can see

Your Burst Pipe Repair Questions Answered

The pipe repair is arranged through licensed plumbers we coordinate with, and it is part of the overall restoration job. What we do ourselves is restore the water damage, meaning we extract, dry and repair every damaged material. Actual plumbing work stays with professionals who hold a plumbing license, and we manage the rest.
Expect 240 to 480 gallons or more every hour from a pipe that has burst. It does not take long for the bill to reach thousands of dollars once floors, walls, ceilings and your possessions get wet. The longer the water flows, the more extensive and costly the damage. That is why a fast response matters so much.
Getting the water out is a 2 to 6 hour job. Structural drying typically takes 3 to 5 days. Depending on how extensive the damage is, full restoration with flooring, paint and drywall repairs can take 1 to 3 weeks. We provide detailed timelines with every project.
Common culprits include corroded or aging lines, pressure that runs too high, physical damage and freezing weather. When water pressure sits over 80 PSI, fittings and pipes are under constant stress. Homes with polybutylene or aging galvanized steel lines face a higher chance of sudden failure. Having a licensed plumber check for corrosion and wear helps you catch problems early.
Most homeowner's insurance policies cover sudden and accidental water damage from burst pipes, including water extraction, drying and repair, though your own policy decides. The pipe repair itself may or may not be covered, depending on your policy. We provide complete documentation for your claim.

Maintenance Tips for Burst Pipe Repair

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Replace old galvanized steel or polybutylene pipes before they fail unexpectedly
  • Outdoor faucets should be closed, drained and free of hoses ahead of freezing weather
  • Find the main shutoff valve now and practice closing it so you can do it fast
  • During freezing weather, keep faucets dripping and cabinet doors open so heat can circulate
  • Never shut the furnace off completely during a winter trip, or pipes inside may freeze
  • Leak detectors placed close to main supply lines and the water heater give early warning
