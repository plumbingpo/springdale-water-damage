How Structural Drying Works in Springdale, AR

Home / Blog / The Science of Structural Drying in Springdale, AR

Few Springdale, AR homeowners know much about structural drying, even though the rest of a restoration job depends on it. Pumping out the standing water still leaves a great deal of moisture inside the insulation, concrete, subfloor, wood framing, and drywall. If this moisture is not properly removed, it will lead to mold growth, material deterioration, and odor problems. Below is a plain look at the science professionals rely on, and at why their results are much better than what DIY drying achieves.

What Psychrometry Means for Drying

Professional structural drying is based on psychrometry, the study of the physical and thermodynamic properties of gas-vapor mixtures. Put simply, it is about the way air movement, relative humidity, and temperature work together to pull water from wet materials. IICRC-certified technicians use psychrometric calculations to determine the equipment setup each drying situation needs, including the type and number of dehumidifiers, the positioning of air movers, and the target temperature and humidity levels.

Commercial Drying Equipment

Professional drying gear is in a different class from anything sold for home use. LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) dehumidifiers can remove 15 to 30 or more pints of moisture per hour from the air, compared to 2 to 3 pints per hour for a residential unit. Evaporation is sped up by air movers that deliver a directed, high-velocity stream of 2,500 CFM or more across wet surfaces. Desiccant dehumidifiers are used for specialized situations, including cold-weather drying and extreme moisture loads. This equipment represents tens of thousands of dollars in investment and requires specific training to deploy effectively.

The Drying Process Step by Step

Professional structural drying follows a systematic process. The opening step is a full assessment: thermal imaging and meters locate and map each wet area. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline moisture readings. Next, psychrometric calculations decide the position of each air mover and dehumidifier so that water evaporates and is removed efficiently. Adding heat is an option when a quicker evaporation rate is needed. A technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment as needed, and document progress. Drying continues until all affected materials reach acceptable moisture levels compared to dry reference standards.

How Many Days Will Drying Take?

With the right commercial equipment, a typical home dries in 3 to 5 days. Several things set the schedule: the amount and kind of equipment in use, the way the building was built, surrounding conditions, the materials involved, with concrete drying much more slowly than drywall, and the quantity of water present. As a rule, standard drywall needs 2 to 3 days, wood framing 3 to 4 days, and concrete 5 to 7 days or more. After the first moisture readings, the technician gives you an estimated schedule and updates it as the job moves along.

The Reason We Monitor Every Day

Without a daily check, drying is only a guess. Only calibrated readings can tell you if a material is drying at all, if it is on pace, and if it has reached a safe moisture level. A wall or floor may seem dry under your hand and still be dangerously wet deeper inside. That kind of proof comes solely from calibrated meters. The drying records show your insurer that industry protocols were followed correctly, which supports the claim and helps protect what the property is worth.
