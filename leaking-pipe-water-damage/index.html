Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Springdale

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. We serve Springdale homeowners by tracing the full wet area and drying every bit of it, including what is out of sight.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. Getting it out is the work we do. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the Water SupplyFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Keep Power AwayWater around a switch, outlet or light fixture means the breaker for that part of the house should go off. Keep your hands off cords and devices that have gotten wet.
  3. Check the Water MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographBefore a repair is made or anything goes in the trash, remove what is stored near the leak and get pictures of the pipe, the staining and the warping.
  5. Have the Area MeteredHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • A nail or screw driven into a pipe during a remodel or while hanging something on the wall.
  • Pinhole corrosion in older copper lines that slowly eats through the pipe wall.
  • A compression fitting or supply line connection at a sink or toilet that has loosened or worn with use.
  • Drain fittings and P-traps below sinks that work loose and leak whenever water goes down.
  • Cold snaps that freeze piping in unheated spaces, leaving small cracks that leak after the thaw.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Ruined Cabinet Bases

Under a sink, a cabinet base made of particleboard puffs up and falls apart after it takes on water. They do not return to shape, so long leaks often mean replacing the base.

Flooring Damage

A leak in a wall or under the slab sends water beneath the floor covering. Laminate seams swell, hardwood boards cup, and the glue holding tile or vinyl releases.

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. Drywall that stays wet goes soft, sags and may need to be cut out.

Soaked Wall Insulation

Insulation in the wall soaks up leak water like a sponge and holds it against the framing. It dries very slowly in a closed cavity, so saturated insulation is usually pulled out.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Check your policy and ask your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.
