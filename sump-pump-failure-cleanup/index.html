Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Springdale: What to Know

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Having passed through the ground, it usually holds bacteria, silt and drain tile residue, which makes it Category 2 at best, or Category 3 once it has been standing or sewage has mixed in. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Stay Out of the WaterNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Our Process

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Pump Out the Basement

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Clean and Sanitize

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. This happens before drying so contaminants are not blown around the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. Make sure a functioning pump is installed again by the time drying wraps up.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Gravel, silt or debris in the pit jamming the impeller or clogging the intake.
  • A failed check valve that lets pumped water run back into the pit.
  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
  • Losing electricity in a storm when the pump has no battery backup.
  • Years of on and off cycling finally burning out an aging motor.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Carpet and Flooring

Groundwater-soaked carpet and padding come out, as cleaning cannot make them safe. Laminate and vinyl plank trap silty water underneath and usually come up too.

Wall Framing

Bottom plates rest directly on concrete, so they are the first framing to get wet. With the wall opened up, this wood is scrubbed, treated and then dried to normal readings.

Finished Basement Walls

Drywall and insulation wick dirty water upward from the slab, well above the visible water line. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

Mechanical equipment often shares the floor with the sump pit. If wiring, burners or control boards got wet they may be ruined, and no appliance should be restarted until it has been checked.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.
