Common Water Damage Issues in Little Rock

Every house has a few weak points where water tends to get loose. These are the ones Little Rock property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Sediment collects in the bottom of a tank and slowly eats through the steel. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. If the tank ruptures, it keeps refilling and draining onto the floor until someone closes the cold supply valve. Heaters in a hallway closet or attic can ruin flooring and ceilings well beyond the utility area.

Roofs: One strong storm is enough to peel back shingles and push water beneath the roof covering. Leaks also start around chimneys, vent pipes and skylights where the sealant has dried out. Water travels along rafters before it drips, so the wet spot inside may be far from the hole outside. On Little Rock roof leak calls, our crew removes saturated insulation, sets drying equipment and can cover the opening until a roofer arrives.

Freezing: Cold nights, depending on the season, can break plumbing that showed no sign of trouble earlier. Outdoor spigots, sprinkler feeds and pipes in crawl spaces or along outside walls are hit first. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: Gurgling sounds and more than one slow fixture at once suggest the main drain is partly blocked. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. Since the waste is hazardous, we extract it and then sanitize concrete, tile and other hard surfaces. Stay out of the affected rooms and avoid running water until the line is cleared.

Seepage: A wet line along the bottom of a foundation wall is a sign that water is building up in the soil next to it. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. White chalky deposits, peeling masonry paint and a persistent damp smell are early warnings. The team at Springdale Restoration can extract standing water and dry the space, and we will tell you what we think let it in.

Simple Ways to Keep a Little Rock Home Dry

A home usually gives a few hints before a leak turns serious. Our technicians see the same early clues again and again in Little Rock, and they are worth learning. Blistering paint, ceiling stains, warped baseboards and floorboards that curl at the edges each point to water behind or beneath them. Add a stale odor in an enclosed space, a loose toilet, or a faucet that keeps dripping.

None of these is an emergency on its own, but each is cheaper to fix now than later. Re-caulk tubs and showers when the bead cracks, since water behind tile does damage you will not see for months. If you have a sump pump, pour a bucket of water into the pit a few times a year to confirm it switches on. Keep stored items off the basement or garage floor on shelves or in plastic bins.

Good upkeep lowers the odds, but it cannot remove them. What it does is make them rarer and smaller, and that matters when the alternative is days of drying equipment in your Little Rock living room.
