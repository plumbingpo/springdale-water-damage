Typical Water Damage Problems in Jonesboro

Walk through a house from the roof down and you pass most of the places a leak can begin. Our crews in Jonesboro follow that same path on an inspection, and these five stops cause the most trouble.

Attics: Rain that slips past a damaged shingle or a loose vent boot lands on the attic floor first. Because insulation absorbs so much, a leak overhead may run through several storms before a mark appears below. Once soaked, that material no longer does its job and holds moisture against the ceiling joists. We take out the ruined material, dry the framing and leave the roof repair itself to a roofer.

Bathrooms: Bathroom floods usually come from a forgotten bathtub, a stopped-up sink or a toilet that will not quit rising. Tile looks waterproof, yet water gets through grout lines and around the edges of the room. In an upstairs bathroom the next stop is the ceiling of the room underneath. Anything that came out of the toilet bowl should be treated as dirty water and cleaned with disinfectant.

Walls: Plumbing hidden in a wall may seep at a joint for weeks without leaving a puddle anywhere. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. Our Jonesboro technicians scan the area with infrared and probe it with a meter to mark the wet zone. Only that section is opened and dried, once a plumber has stopped the leak.

Tanks: A water heater holds a large volume under pressure, usually in a closet, garage or utility room. Drips from the relief valve, the drain valve or the fittings on top are early signs that parts are wearing. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit. We pump out the spill and test the surrounding floor and wall bases, since water tends to run under them.

Drains: The lowest drain in the house is where a sewer blockage shows itself. Dark water rising in a floor drain or a ground-level shower means the main line is not carrying waste away. That water is Category 3, the most contaminated kind, and it takes protective equipment to clean up safely. Stop using water in the house and keep people and pets away until a trained crew arrives in Jonesboro.
